## How Transformers Work
Transformers are devices that transfer electrical energy from one circuit to another through electromagnetic induction. The principle of electromagnetic induction, [Not found in provided sources](#), states that an electric current is generated in a conductor when it is exposed to a changing magnetic field. This principle is the foundation of transformer operation.
In a transformer, there are two coils: the primary coil and the secondary coil. The primary coil is connected to an alternating current (AC) source, while the secondary coil is connected to a load. When an AC current flows through the primary coil, it generates a magnetic field that induces a voltage in the secondary coil. The role of the primary coil is to produce the magnetic field, and the role of the secondary coil is to capture this field and produce an output voltage.
The turns ratio, which is the ratio of the number of turns in the primary coil to the number of turns in the secondary coil, determines the voltage transformation. The turns ratio is crucial in transformers as it allows for the adjustment of voltage levels, enabling the efficient transmission and distribution of electricity over long distances. For example, a step-up transformer can increase the voltage of the input signal, while a step-down transformer can decrease it.
